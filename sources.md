## Structured bibliography (2026-09-23)

The inline reference list was migrated to references.yaml (CSL) and in-text citations to Pandoc [@id] syntax. Five of the 23 entries carry DOIs resolved through doi.org content negotiation and checked for title, authors and year: Abusch (2015), Fowler (2004), Mauss (1938), Russo and Simon (1968) and Strathern (1988). The other 18 were entered by hand; the automatic Crossref match for Pariser (2011) was a book review and was replaced by the Penguin Press book.
The legacy list gave the classical and early-modern works as "(Various translations.)". The ancient and medieval works, previously cited by name only, are now cited to standard English translations at the passages the text discusses: Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*, trans. Irwin (Hackett, 1999); Augustine, *Confessions*, trans. Chadwick (Oxford, 1991); Boethius, *Contra Eutychen et Nestorium*, ch. 3, in the Loeb *Theological Tractates* (1973); Gaius, *Institutes* 1.8 and 1.9, trans. Gordon and Robinson (Duckworth, 1988), whose English wording of the two quoted sentences may differ slightly from the paper's rendering; Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 29, a. 1, trans. Fathers of the English Dominican Province (1920). Descartes, Locke, Hume, Kant and Hegel remain cited by original publication year without a specific edition. Foucault's three books are cited as the Gallimard originals under their French titles, since the legacy entries had paired English titles with the French publisher and year.
Corrections: Abusch (2015) is the book *Male and Female in the Epic of Gilgamesh: Encounters, Literary History, and Interpretation* (Eisenbrauns); no chapter titled "The development of the Epic of Gilgamesh" exists in it (the nearest is "The Development and Meaning of the Epic of Gilgamesh: An Interpretive Essay"), so the book is cited. The legacy entry "Russo, J. (2012). Re-thinking Homeric psychology. In F. Montanari (Ed.), *Homeric Contexts*. De Gruyter" could not be confirmed: the Crossref table of contents of *Homeric Contexts* (10.1515/9783110272017) contains no Russo chapter, and no 2012 Russo article with that title was found in Crossref or OpenAlex. The sentence that quoted it ("fundamentally misguided") was rewritten to cite Russo and Simon (1968), *Journal of the History of Ideas* 29(4), which explains Homeric representation of mental life through oral composition in dialogue with Snell. Kahan et al. (2017), Friston (2013) and Assmann (1997) were in the legacy list but were never cited and no passage in the text draws on them; they were removed.
Not re-verified: the Fowler (2004) and Abusch (2015) quotations (the books' abstracts support the paraphrase, not the wording).

Russo (2012) is cited from Quaderni Urbinati di Cultura Classica n.s. 101(2), 11-28 (JSTOR 23347416; Haverford College repository record); the venue in the legacy list (Montanari, Homeric Contexts) was wrong. The quoted phrase was not checked against the full text.
