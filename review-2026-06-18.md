# Review: invention-of-the-person

## Verdict
**Reject, with an invitation to resubmit as a major revision.** The prose is genuinely excellent and the history-of-philosophy spine (Gaius, Boethius, Locke, Hume, Kant, Hegel) is rendered accurately and with real economy. But the paper's central thesis — that "person" is a historically assembled category that gets retroactively naturalized — is, almost beat for beat, **Marcel Mauss's famous 1938 argument** ("Une catégorie de l'esprit humain: la notion de personne"), which is nowhere cited or acknowledged; nor is Charles Taylor's *Sources of the Self*, the standard genealogy of modern identity. A paper that re-derives a canonical thesis while presenting it as fresh, and that grounds its contemporary payoff (§8) in zero citations and a self-admittedly stipulative simulation, cannot clear a top venue's originality and rigor bars in its current form. The single most serious problem is the uncited collision with Mauss/Taylor: it converts the paper's "contribution" from discovery into competent restatement.

## Claim grounding

- "every number in §9 is a key in that file" / all headline decimals — **Supported.** I re-ran `analyses.py`: 0.39, 0.93, 2.4×, 14, 0.36, 9.5, >25×, Gini 0.13→0.61, span 4,075 yr, gaps 1,650/1,169 yr all reproduce exactly from `results.json`. Spot-check passes; the prose describes the model the code implements.
- "An infrastructural human… scores 0.93 practically. The practical personhood of the two grades differs by a factor of about 2.4" (§9.2) — **Supported as arithmetic, but circular as evidence.** The paper is admirably honest that "the model inherits this asymmetry from the section's claims rather than discovering it." The 2.4× and 26× figures are deterministic functions of hand-chosen inputs (support vectors 0.4/0.5/0.3…; reciprocation probabilities 0.05/0.04). They are restatement, not measurement, and the "26-to-1" ratio is especially sensitive to an arbitrary 0.05 constant. The disclosure mitigates; the deployment of precise-looking numbers in the abstract does not.
- "the practical conditions… are being eroded for large portions of the population while being concentrated in a small one" (§8.1) — **Unsupported.** This is the paper's contemporary thesis and it carries **no citation and no data**. The entire profiled/infrastructural argument (§8.1–8.2) is asserted; Zuboff's *Age of Surveillance Capitalism* — the obvious load-bearing source — is absent.
- "The discovery that *Homo naledi* practiced intentional interment has pushed these questions further back in time" (§2.2) — **Overstated.** The 2023 Berger claims are strongly contested; an eLife reviewed-preprint and a *Journal of Human Evolution* rebuttal hold there is "no scientific evidence" of deliberate burial. Stating it as "the discovery that [they] practiced intentional interment" presents a disputed interpretation as established fact. ("reinvigorated debates" softens it, but the noun phrase asserts the conclusion.)
- "Much of what we mean by 'identity'… owes more to this epic layer than to Kant or to Roman law" (§3.3) — **Unsupported causal claim.** A strong genealogical attribution offered without argument or evidence.
- "When all of these layers collapse together… then and only then does the contemporary Person appear" (§10) — **Asserted, not demonstrated.** The fusion of the five layers into one self-identical concept is the hinge of the whole paper and is never shown historically; it is stipulated.

## Argument

- **§1 / §10 — the load-bearing inference is a non-sequitur.** "The Person was assembled in layers, and what was assembled can be disassembled." Historical assembly does not entail present decomposability (a synthesized compound is "assembled" but not freely "disassembled"). The thesis treats the move as self-evident; it is the paper's central claim and it is not earned.
- **Originality — uncited collision with the foundational literature.** Mauss (1938) already argued that "la personne" is a historically constructed category, not a natural kind; Charles Taylor (*Sources of the Self*, 1989) already supplied the canonical layered genealogy of the modern self. Their absence is not a citation nicety — it means the paper's framing thesis is presented as novel when it is canonical.
- **§8 — the payoff section is the weakest, evidentially.** The "profiled vs. infrastructural" grades and "thinning, not exclusion" reframing are the genuinely useful ideas in the paper, but they are delivered as pure assertion: no Zuboff, no data on algorithmic management, no empirical anchor. For a venue demanding rigor, the most original section is the least grounded.
- **§9 — quantitative veneer over stipulation.** The simulation is honestly labeled as a consistency check, but the abstract and §9 prose ("differ by a factor of about 2.4," "more than twenty-five to one") let stipulated constants read as findings. A skeptical reader is owed a sentence in the abstract, not just §9, that these are illustrations not measurements.
- **The stratigraphy metaphor is asserted, not defended.** "Layer," "beneath," "persists beneath all of them" treat the geological metaphor as literal (literally so in §9.1). The live alternative — that relational/dividual personhood is a *rival* to the bounded individual rather than a *substratum* of it — is never addressed. The paper papers over the possibility that these are competing, not cumulative, formations.
- **Standalone-ness / scope.** §8.3 explicitly depends on the companion "(PIATRA Institute, 2026)" *Epistemic Lensing*, which is unpublished here; that subsection does not stand alone. The spine is also an entirely Western canon (Gilgamesh→Foucault) delivered in universalizing language about "the modern Person," with the one non-Western layer (relational/Melanesian) bracketed as prehistory.
- **Genuinely strong:** the §8.1 distinction between *exclusion from the category* (19th-c. problem) and *emptying of the category from within* (21st-c. problem) is a crisp, real reframing; the Locke "forensic term" reading and the Hume→Kant "crisis and resolution" framing are accurate and well-told; and the §9 epistemic-status honesty ("none of them measures history") is exactly right and rare.

## Factual errors

- **Augustine "writing in the 5th century"** (§5.2): the *Confessions*, the work discussed (divided will, Book 8), was written ~397–401 CE — late **4th** century. Augustine's 5th-century output is the later anti-Pelagian and *City of God* material. Minor imprecision.
- **Shamhat "the temple prostitute"** (§3.1): the epic calls her a *ḫarimtu* (harlot); the specifically *temple/sacred* prostitution reading is contested among Assyriologists. Overspecified. Minor.
- **"full *status capitis*"** (§4.1): non-standard. Roman status is analyzed as *libertatis / civitatis / familiae*; *caput*/*capitis deminutio* is the related term. Loose but not wrong in spirit. Minor.
- The *Homo naledi* statement (above, in Claim grounding) is the one factual claim I would call an actual error of assertion rather than imprecision.
- No fabricated dates or invented works detected in the load-bearing classical/philosophical citations; those are sound.

## Prose and insight

**Alive:** the opening of §1 ("It governs itself. It can deliberate, reflect, and choose…") earns its rhetoric; §4.1's account of the *persona* as "a bureaucratic technology for organizing liability" is genuinely sharp; the §8.1–8.2 reframing (formal inclusion with practical hollowing; profiled vs. infrastructural) is the paper's best idea, the place where it stops summarizing and starts thinking. The §5 confession-to-surveillance arc ("This layer makes confession possible, but it also makes surveillance desirable") is the kind of line that justifies the genre.

**Dead:** §6.5, §3.3, §4.2, and the other "The Contribution of This Layer" codas are mechanical — each restates what the section just said in a flatter register; they read as scaffolding the author forgot to remove. §7.3's arrow-diagram is a table of contents masquerading as synthesis. §8.5 (neurotechnology) is a competent listicle of split-brain/DID/psychedelics/uploading that any educated reader could assemble, and adds no argument the genealogy needs.

**Genuine contribution, if any:** the reframing of the contemporary threat as *thinning from within rather than exclusion from the category*, paired with the profiled/infrastructural distinction. That is worth a paper. But it sits in the least-supported section and is partially anticipated by surveillance-capitalism literature. The genealogy itself is elegant pedagogy, not a new finding.

## Top fixes

1. **Confront Mauss (1938) and Taylor (1989) head-on.** Locate the paper's claim relative to the texts that already made it, and state precisely what is new (candidate: the *thinning-not-exclusion* + profiled/infrastructural analysis, and the stratigraphic-supports model). Without this the originality claim fails.
2. **Ground §8.** It is the real contribution and currently has zero citations. Add Zuboff and the algorithmic-management/platform-power literature, and at least gesture at evidence for "eroded for large populations… concentrated in a small one." Assertion is not argument at this altitude.
3. **Fix the Russo citation and the two unverifiable quotes.** Correct Russo to the *QUCC* (Fabrizio Serra) 2012 article; verify or delete "fundamentally misguided"; supply a real source for the "scars" quotation or cut it.
4. **Soften the *Homo naledi* claim to match the evidence.** Replace "The discovery that *Homo naledi* practiced intentional interment" with language reflecting that the burial interpretation is contested.
5. **Clean the bibliography.** Remove or actually use Assmann, Friston, Kahan, Pariser, Sunstein; credit Marriott (1976) for "dividual."
6. **Defend the inferential hinge and the metaphor.** Give an actual argument for "assembled → can be disassembled," and address the rival view that relational personhood is a competitor to, not a substratum of, the bounded individual.
7. **Re-status the simulation in the abstract.** State once, up front, that §9's figures are illustrations of stipulated structure, not measurements — so "2.4×" and "26-to-1" are not mistaken for empirical results.
8. **Cut or rewrite the "Contribution of This Layer" codas and §8.5** to remove restatement and the destabilization listicle; make standalone the §8.3 material now dependent on the unpublished companion paper.
